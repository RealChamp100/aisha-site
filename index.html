A simple truth

What looks like a people problem is often a system problem.

Whatever you just ticked, it's rarely random. Culture isn't created by values statements alone. People learn it by watching what leaders do, the agreements they follow and the processes they use.

When the system stays the same, old habits return. So I look at the three connected parts shaping how work actually gets done.

"How we do things around here." I start with one question: does this serve everyone it's supposed to serve?

Who it's for

For the people asked to lead, without the authority to make anyone follow.

If your most capable people keep hitting the same wall — plenty of responsibility, not enough leverage — this is built for them.

Network & ERG leaders

Running employee networks or resource groups on top of a day job, trying to create real change across the business.

Initiative & project leads

Driving cross-functional work where the people they depend on report to someone else entirely.

Emerging leaders

Stepping into influence before they have positional power, and needing to build credibility fast.

Change champions

Asked to carry a transformation into their teams and win hearts, not just issue instructions.

The problem it solves

Commitment isn't the bottleneck. Knowing how to move people is.

Most people asked to lead through influence are motivated and capable. What they're missing isn't will — it's a repeatable way to build trust, frame the ask, and bring others along when they can't simply direct them.
